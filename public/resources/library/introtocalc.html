Introduction to Limits

The concept of a limit is fundamental to calculus and mathematical analysis. Intuitively, the limit of a function describes the behavior of that function near a particular input.

Definition: Limit

We say that the limit of $f(x)$ as $x$ approaches $a$ is $L$, written as:

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$$

if for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a $\delta > 0$ such that whenever $0 < |x - a| < \delta$, we have $|f(x) - L| < \varepsilon$.

Formal Definition of Limits

The formal definition of limits, known as the epsilon-delta definition, provides a rigorous mathematical framework for understanding limits.

Theorem: Epsilon-Delta Definition

Let $f$ be a function defined on some open interval that contains the number $a$, except possibly at $a$ itself. Then we say that the limit of $f(x)$ as $x$ approaches $a$ is $L$, and we write:

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$$

if for every number $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a number $\delta > 0$ such that:

$$\text{if } 0 < |x - a| < \delta \text{ then } |f(x) - L| < \varepsilon$$

Limit Laws

The following laws allow us to compute limits of complicated functions in terms of limits of simpler functions.

Theorem: Basic Limit Laws

Suppose that $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ and $\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = M$. Then:

  1. $\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) + g(x)] = L + M$ (Sum Law)
  2. $\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) - g(x)] = L - M$ (Difference Law)
  3. $\lim_{x \to a} [cf(x)] = cL$ where $c$ is a constant (Constant Multiple Law)
  4. $\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) \cdot g(x)] = L \cdot M$ (Product Law)
  5. $\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{L}{M}$ if $M \neq 0$ (Quotient Law)
Example 1

Find $\lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 + 2x - 1)$.

Solution:

Using the limit laws:

\begin{align} \lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 + 2x - 1) &= \lim_{x \to 2} 3x^2 + \lim_{x \to 2} 2x - \lim_{x \to 2} 1 \\ &= 3 \lim_{x \to 2} x^2 + 2 \lim_{x \to 2} x - 1 \\ &= 3(2)^2 + 2(2) - 1 \\ &= 12 + 4 - 1 \\ &= 15 \end{align}

Continuity

A function is continuous at a point if the limit exists and equals the function value at that point.

Definition: Continuity at a Point

A function $f$ is continuous at a number $a$ if:

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$$

This definition implicitly requires three things:

  1. $f(a)$ is defined
  2. $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ exists
  3. $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$
Important Note

If any of the three conditions for continuity fail, then the function is discontinuous at that point. Common types of discontinuities include removable discontinuities, jump discontinuities, and infinite discontinuities.

Additional Examples

Example 2: One-Sided Limits

Consider the function $f(x) = \frac{|x|}{x}$. Find the left and right limits as $x$ approaches 0.

Solution:

For $x > 0$: $|x| = x$, so $f(x) = \frac{x}{x} = 1$

For $x < 0$: $|x|=-x$, so $f(x)=\frac{-x}{x}=-1$

\begin{align} \lim_{x \to 0^+} f(x) &= 1 \\ \lim_{x \to 0^-} f(x) &= -1 \end{align}

Since the left and right limits are different, $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x)$ does not exist.

Practice Exercises

Work through these exercises to test your understanding:

  1. Find $\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3}$
  2. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x}$
  3. Determine if $f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 & \text{if } x \leq 1 \\ 2x - 1 & \text{if } x > 1 \end{cases}$ is continuous at $x = 1$
Hint for Exercise 1

Factor the numerator and simplify before taking the limit. Remember that $x^2 - 9 = (x-3)(x+3)$.
