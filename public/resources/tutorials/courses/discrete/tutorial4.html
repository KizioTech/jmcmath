Introduction

The pigeonhole principle is a fundamental concept in discrete mathematics that states that if you have more items than containers, at least one container must contain at least two items. This principle is often used to solve problems in combinatorics and probability.

Key Point: The pigeonhole principle is a fundamental concept in discrete mathematics that states that if you have more items than containers, at least one container must contain at least two items.

PRODUCT RULE

The product rule is a fundamental counting technique that allows us to count the number of ways to choose two or more items from a set. It is used to count the number of ways to choose two items from a set, the number of ways to choose three items from a set, and so on.

Key Point: The product rule is a fundamental counting technique that allows us to count the number of ways to choose two or more items from a set.
Problem 1
How many different ways can you choose two items from a set of 5 items?

Since both the base and the exponent involve the variable $x$, we use logarithmic differentiation.

  1. Take the natural logarithm of both sides of the equation.:
    $$ \ln y = \ln(x^x) $$
  2. Apply the logarithm property $\ln(a^b) = b \ln a$ to the right side.
    $$ \ln y = x \ln x $$
  3. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$. The left side, $\ln y$, requires implicit differentiation. The right side, $x \ln x$, requires the product rule, where $(uv)' = u'v + uv'. Let $u=x$ and $v=\ln x$.
    $$ \frac{d}{dx}(\ln y) = \frac{d}{dx}(x \ln x) $$ $$ \frac{1}{y} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} = \left(\frac{d}{dx}(x)\right) \ln x + x \left(\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x)\right) $$ $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = (1) \cdot \ln x + x \cdot \left(\frac{1}{x}\right) $$ $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \ln x + 1 $$
  4. Solve for $\frac{dy}{dx}$ by multiplying both sides by $y$.
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = y (\ln x + 1) $$
  5. Substitute the original expression for $y$ (which is $y=x^x$) back into the equation.
    $$\therefore \frac{dy}{dx} = x^x (\ln x + 1) $$
  6. Thus, the derivative of $y=x^x$ is $x^x(1 + \ln x)$.
Problem 2
Find $\displaystyle \frac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = \ln(x^2 + 1)^{\sin x}$.
  1. Let $y = \ln(x^2 + 1)^{\sin x}$. Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
    $$ \ln y = \sin x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) $$
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
    $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \cos x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) + \sin x \cdot \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} $$
  3. Multiply both sides by $y$ and substitute $y = (x^2 + 1)^{\sin x}$:
    $$ \therefore \frac{dy}{dx} = (x^2 + 1)^{\sin x} \left[ \cos x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) + \sin x \cdot \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} \right] $$
