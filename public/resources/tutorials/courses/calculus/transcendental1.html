Introduction

Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

Exponential functions are of the form $y = e^x$, where $e$ is the base of the natural logarithm. Logarithmic functions are the inverses of exponential functions, of the form $y = \ln(x)$.

Key Point: Exponential functions are essential in calculus and have many applications in various fields. Logarithmic functions are the inverses of exponential functions, of the form $y = \ln(x)$.

Logarithmic Differentiation

Logarithmic differentiation is a technique used to differentiate functions that are expressed as powers of other functions. It is particularly useful when the function is a product or quotient of two or more functions, or when the function is a power of a function.

The technique involves taking the natural logarithm of both sides of the equation and then differentiating both sides with respect to the variable. This allows us to avoid using the product rule or quotient rule, which can be complex and time-consuming.

Here are the steps for logarithmic differentiation:

  1. Take the natural logarithm of both sides of the equation.
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to the variable.
Problem 1
Differentiate the function $y = x^x$ with respect to $x$.

Since both the base and the exponent involve the variable $x$, we use logarithmic differentiation.

  1. Take the natural logarithm of both sides of the equation.:
    $$ \ln y = \ln(x^x) $$
  2. Apply the logarithm property $\ln(a^b) = b \ln a$ to the right side.
    $$ \ln y = x \ln x $$
  3. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$. The left side, $\ln y$, requires implicit differentiation. The right side, $x \ln x$, requires the product rule, where $(uv)' = u'v + uv'. Let $u=x$ and $v=\ln x$.
    $$ \frac{d}{dx}(\ln y) = \frac{d}{dx}(x \ln x) $$ $$ \frac{1}{y} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} = \left(\frac{d}{dx}(x)\right) \ln x + x \left(\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x)\right) $$ $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = (1) \cdot \ln x + x \cdot \left(\frac{1}{x}\right) $$ $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \ln x + 1 $$
  4. Solve for $\frac{dy}{dx}$ by multiplying both sides by $y$.
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = y (\ln x + 1) $$
  5. Substitute the original expression for $y$ (which is $y=x^x$) back into the equation.
    $$\therefore \frac{dy}{dx} = x^x (\ln x + 1) $$
  6. Thus, the derivative of $y=x^x$ is $x^x(1 + \ln x)$.
Problem 2
Find $\displaystyle \frac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = \ln(x^2 + 1)^{\sin x}$.
  1. Let $y = \ln(x^2 + 1)^{\sin x}$. Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
    $$ \ln y = \sin x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) $$
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
    $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \cos x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) + \sin x \cdot \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} $$
  3. Multiply both sides by $y$ and substitute $y = (x^2 + 1)^{\sin x}$:
    $$ \therefore \frac{dy}{dx} = (x^2 + 1)^{\sin x} \left[ \cos x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) + \sin x \cdot \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} \right] $$
Problem 3
Differentiate the function $y = (x^2 + 1)^{\tan x}$ with respect to $x$.
  1. Let $y = (x^2 + 1)^{\tan x}$. Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
    $$ \ln y = \tan x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) $$
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
    $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \sec^2 x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) + \tan x \cdot \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} $$
  3. Multiply both sides by $y$ and substitute $y = (x^2 + 1)^{\tan x}$:
    $$\therefore \frac{dy}{dx} = (x^2 + 1)^{\tan x} \left[ \sec^2 x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) + \tan x \cdot \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} \right] $$
Problem 4
Find $\displaystyle \frac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = \frac{(x^2 + 3)^5}{(2x - 1)^4}$.
  1. Let $y = \frac{(x^2 + 3)^5}{(2x - 1)^4}$. Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
    $$ \ln y = 5 \ln(x^2 + 3) - 4 \ln(2x - 1) $$
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
    $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = 5 \cdot \frac{2x}{x^2 + 3} - 4 \cdot \frac{2}{2x - 1} $$
  3. Multiply both sides by $y$:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{(x^2 + 3)^5}{(2x - 1)^4} \left( \frac{10x}{x^2 + 3} - \frac{8}{2x - 1} \right) $$
Problem 5
Differentiate $y = x^2 \sqrt{x+1} \, e^{3x}$ with respect to $x$.
  1. Let $y = x^2 \sqrt{x+1} \, e^{3x}$. Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
    $$ \ln y = 2\ln x + \frac{1}{2}\ln(x+1) + 3x $$
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
    $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2}{x} + \frac{1}{2(x+1)} + 3 $$
  3. Multiply both sides by $y$:
    $$\therefore \frac{dy}{dx} = x^2 \sqrt{x+1} \, e^{3x} \left( \frac{2}{x} + \frac{1}{2(x+1)} + 3 \right) $$
Problem 6
Find $\displaystyle \frac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = (x^{\sin x})^{\cos x}$.
  1. Let $y = (x^{\sin x})^{\cos x}$. Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
    $$ \ln y = \cos x \cdot \ln(x^{\sin x}) $$
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
    $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \cos x \cdot \ln(x^{\sin x}) + \sin x \cdot \frac{1}{x} $$
  3. Multiply both sides by $y$:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = (x^{\sin x})^{\cos x} \left[ \cos x \cdot \ln(x^{\sin x}) + \sin x \cdot \frac{1}{x} \right] $$
Problem 7
Find $\displaystyle \frac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = \ln(\sin(\ln x))$.
  1. Let $y = \ln(\sin(\ln x))$. Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
    $$ \ln y = \ln(\sin(\ln x)) $$
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
    $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sin(\ln x)} \cdot \cos(\ln x) \cdot \frac{1}{x} $$
  3. Multiply both sides by $y$:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sin(\ln x)} \cdot \cos(\ln x) \cdot \frac{1}{x} $$
Problem 8
Find $\displaystyle \frac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = e^{x \ln x}$.
  1. Rewrite $y = e^{x \ln x} = x^x$.
    $$ \ln y = x \ln x $$
  2. We have already differentiated $y = x^x$ in Problem 1, but let's show the steps:
    $$ \ln y = x \ln x $$
  3. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
    $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \ln x + 1 $$
  4. Multiply both sides by $y$:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = x^x (\ln x + 1) $$
Problem 9
Find $\displaystyle \frac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = \ln(x^2 + 1)^{\sin x}$.
  1. Let $y = \ln(x^2 + 1)^{\sin x}$. Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
    $$ \ln y = \sin x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) $$
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
    $$ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \cos x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) + \sin x \cdot \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} $$
  3. Multiply both sides by $y$ and substitute $y = (x^2 + 1)^{\sin x}$:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = (x^2 + 1)^{\sin x} \left[ \cos x \cdot \ln(x^2 + 1) + \sin x \cdot \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} \right] $$
Problem 10
Find $\displaystyle \frac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = \ln(\sin(\ln x))$.
  1. Let $y = \ln(\sin(\ln x))$.
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sin(\ln x)} \cdot \cos(\ln x) \cdot \frac{1}{x} $$

Logarithmic Integration

Logarithmic integration is a technique used to evaluate integrals that involve logarithmic functions. It is particularly useful when dealing with integrals of the form $\int \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \, dx$, where $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are functions of $x$. By using integration by parts or substitution, we can simplify the integral and find its value.

Problem 1
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{x} \, dx$.
  1. We can use the property of logarithmic integration:
    $$ \int \frac{1}{x} \, dx = \ln |x| + C $$
Problem 2
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} \, dx$.
  1. Let $u = x^2 + 1$, so $du = 2x\,dx$.
    $$ \int \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} \, dx = \int \frac{1}{u} \, du = \ln|u| + C = \ln|x^2 + 1| + C $$
Problem 3
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{3x+2} \, dx$.
  1. Let $u = 3x + 2$, so $du = 3\,dx$, $dx = \frac{du}{3}$.
    $$ \int \frac{1}{3x+2} \, dx = \int \frac{1}{u} \cdot \frac{du}{3} = \frac{1}{3} \ln|u| + C = \frac{1}{3} \ln|3x+2| + C $$
Problem 4
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{5}{2x-7} \, dx$.
  1. Let $u = 2x - 7$, so $du = 2\,dx$, $dx = \frac{du}{2}$.
    $$ \int \frac{5}{2x-7} \, dx = 5 \int \frac{1}{u} \cdot \frac{du}{2} = \frac{5}{2} \ln|u| + C = \frac{5}{2} \ln|2x-7| + C $$
Problem 5
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{x^2-4} \, dx$.
  1. Factor denominator: $x^2-4 = (x-2)(x+2)$. Use partial fractions:
    $$ \frac{1}{x^2-4} = \frac{A}{x-2} + \frac{B}{x+2} $$
  2. Solve for $A$ and $B$:
    $$ 1 = A(x+2) + B(x-2) $$
  3. Let $x=2$: $1 = A(4) \implies A = \frac{1}{4}$
    $$ \int \frac{1}{x^2-4} dx = \frac{1}{4} \int \frac{1}{x-2} dx - \frac{1}{4} \int \frac{1}{x+2} dx $$
  4. Let $x=-2$: $1 = B(-4) \implies B = -\frac{1}{4}$
    $$ = \frac{1}{4} \ln|x-2| - \frac{1}{4} \ln|x+2| + C $$
Problem 6
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{3x+5}{x^2+5x+6} \, dx$.
  1. Factor denominator: $x^2+5x+6 = (x+2)(x+3)$. Use partial fractions:
    $$ \frac{3x+5}{(x+2)(x+3)} = \frac{A}{x+2} + \frac{B}{x+3} $$
  2. Multiply both sides by $(x+2)(x+3)$:
    $$ 3x+5 = A(x+3) + B(x+2) $$
  3. Let $x=-3$: $3(-3)+5 = A(0) + B(-1) \implies -9+5 = -B \implies B = 4$
    $$ \int \frac{3x+5}{x^2+5x+6} dx = \int \frac{-1}{x+2} dx + \int \frac{4}{x+3} dx $$
  4. Let $x=-2$: $3(-2)+5 = A(1) + B(0) \implies -6+5 = A \implies A = -1$
    $$ = -\ln|x+2| + 4\ln|x+3| + C $$
Problem 7
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{2x+1}{x^2+x} \, dx$.
  1. Factor denominator: $x^2+x = x(x+1)$. Use partial fractions:
    $$ \frac{2x+1}{x(x+1)} = \frac{A}{x} + \frac{B}{x+1} $$
  2. Multiply both sides by $x(x+1)$:
    $$ 2x+1 = A(x+1) + Bx $$
  3. Let $x=0$: $1 = A(1) \implies A=1$
    $$ \int \frac{2x+1}{x^2+x} dx = \int \frac{1}{x} dx + \int \frac{1}{x+1} dx $$
  4. Let $x=-1$: $2(-1)+1 = B(-1) \implies -2+1 = -B \implies B=1$
    $$ = \ln|x| + \ln|x+1| + C $$
Problem 8
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{4x-1}{2x^2-x} \, dx$.
  1. Factor denominator: $2x^2-x = x(2x-1)$. Use partial fractions:
    $$ \frac{4x-1}{x(2x-1)} = \frac{A}{x} + \frac{B}{2x-1} $$
  2. Multiply both sides by $x(2x-1)$:
    $$ 4x-1 = A(2x-1) + Bx $$
  3. Let $x=0$: $-1 = A(-1) \implies A=1$
    $$ \int \frac{4x-1}{2x^2-x} dx = \int \frac{1}{x} dx + \int \frac{2}{2x-1} dx $$
  4. Let $x=\frac{1}{2}$: $4(\frac{1}{2})-1 = B(\frac{1}{2}) \implies 2-1 = \frac{B}{2} \implies B=2$
    $$ = \ln|x| + \int \frac{2}{2x-1} dx $$
  5. Let $u=2x-1$, $du=2dx$, so $dx = du/2$:
    $$ \int \frac{2}{2x-1} dx = \int \frac{2}{u} \cdot \frac{du}{2} = \int \frac{1}{u} du = \ln|u| + C = \ln|2x-1| + C $$
  6. Let $u=2x-1$, $du=2dx$, so $dx = du/2$:
    $$ = \ln|x| + \ln|2x-1| + C $$
Problem 9
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{x^2+4x+3} \, dx$.
  1. Factor denominator: $x^2+4x+3 = (x+1)(x+3)$. Use partial fractions:
    $$ \frac{1}{(x+1)(x+3)} = \frac{A}{x+1} + \frac{B}{x+3} $$
  2. Multiply both sides by $(x+1)(x+3)$:
    $$ 1 = A(x+3) + B(x+1) $$
  3. Let $x=-3$: $1 = A(0) + B(-2) \implies B = -\frac{1}{2}$
    $$ \int \frac{1}{x^2+4x+3} dx = \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{1}{x+1} dx - \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{1}{x+3} dx $$
  4. Let $x=-1$: $1 = A(2) + B(0) \implies A = \frac{1}{2}$
    $$ = \frac{1}{2} \ln|x+1| - \frac{1}{2} \ln|x+3| + C $$
Problem 10
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{2x+3}{x^2+3x+2} \, dx$.
  1. Factor denominator: $x^2+3x+2 = (x+1)(x+2)$. Use partial fractions:
    $$ \frac{2x+3}{(x+1)(x+2)} = \frac{A}{x+1} + \frac{B}{x+2} $$
  2. Multiply both sides by $(x+1)(x+2)$:
    $$ 2x+3 = A(x+2) + B(x+1) $$
  3. Let $x=-2$: $2(-2)+3 = A(0) + B(-1) \implies -4+3 = -B \implies B=1$
    $$ \int \frac{2x+3}{x^2+3x+2} dx = \int \frac{1}{x+1} dx + \int \frac{1}{x+2} dx $$
  4. Let $x=-1$: $2(-1)+3 = A(1) + B(0) \implies -2+3 = A \implies A=1$
    $$ = \ln|x+1| + \ln|x+2| + C $$
Problem 11
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{x^2-2x} \, dx$.
  1. Factor denominator: $x^2-2x = x(x-2)$. Use partial fractions:
    $$ \frac{1}{x(x-2)} = \frac{A}{x} + \frac{B}{x-2} $$
  2. Multiply both sides by $x(x-2)$:
    $$ 1 = A(x-2) + Bx $$
  3. Let $x=0$: $1 = A(-2) \implies A = -\frac{1}{2}$
    $$ \int \frac{1}{x^2-2x} dx = -\frac{1}{2} \int \frac{1}{x} dx + \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{1}{x-2} dx $$
  4. Let $x=2$: $1 = B(2) \implies B = \frac{1}{2}$
    $$ = -\frac{1}{2} \ln|x| + \frac{1}{2} \ln|x-2| + C $$
Problem 12
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{3x+2}{x^2+2x} \, dx$.
  1. Factor denominator: $x^2+2x = x(x+2)$. Use partial fractions:
    $$ \frac{3x+2}{x(x+2)} = \frac{A}{x} + \frac{B}{x+2} $$
  2. Multiply both sides by $x(x+2)$:
    $$ 3x+2 = A(x+2) + Bx $$
  3. Let $x=-2$: $3(-2)+2 = B(-2) \implies -6+2 = -2B \implies B=2$
    $$ \int \frac{3x+2}{x^2+2x} dx = \int \frac{1}{x} dx + \int \frac{2}{x+2} dx $$
  4. Let $x=0$: $2 = A(2) \implies A=1$
    $$ = \ln|x| + 2\ln|x+2| + C $$
Problem 13
Evaluate the definite integral $\displaystyle \int_{1}^{e} \frac{1}{x} \, dx$.
  1. The antiderivative is $\ln|x|$. Thus,
    $$ \int_{1}^{e} \frac{1}{x} \, dx = \left[ \ln|x| \right]_{1}^{e} = \ln e - \ln 1 = 1 - 0 = 1 $$
Problem 14
Evaluate the definite integral $\displaystyle \int_{0}^{1} \frac{2}{x+2} \, dx$.
  1. Let $u = x+2$, $du = dx$. The antiderivative is $2\ln|x+2|$.
    $$ \int_{0}^{1} \frac{2}{x+2} \, dx = \left[ 2\ln|x+2| \right]_{0}^{1} = 2\ln 3 - 2\ln 2 = 2\ln\left(\frac{3}{2}\right) $$
Problem 15
Evaluate the definite integral $\displaystyle \int_{1}^{2} \frac{1}{x^2} \, dx$.
  1. The antiderivative is $-\frac{1}{x}$.
    $$ \int_{1}^{2} \frac{1}{x^2} \, dx = \left[ -\frac{1}{x} \right]_{1}^{2} = -\frac{1}{2} - (-1) = 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2} $$

Inverse Trigonometric Functions

Inverse trigonometric functions are the inverse functions of the trigonometric functions. They are used to find the angle when the sine, cosine, or tangent of an angle is known.

  • Mathematically, the six basic trigonometric functions do not have inverses because their graphs repeat periodically and do not pass the horizontal line test.
  • However, inverses of trigonometric functions are widely used in finding angles that are associated with specific ratios. These inverses usually give angles which are in the range \(-\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\). For example, consider \[ \boxed{ \sin x = \frac{1}{2}\text{;} \quad x = \sin^{-1} \frac{1}{2}= 30^\circ} \]

Derivatives of Inverse Trigonometric Functions

The derivatives of inverse trigonometric functions are important in calculus and have various applications in mathematics and physics. Below are some problems and solutions related to the derivatives of inverse trigonometric functions for your practice.

Problem 1
Find $\displaystyle \frac{d}{dx} \arcsin(2x)$.
  1. Let $y = \arcsin(2x)$. By the chain rule:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(2x)^2}} \cdot 2 = \frac{2}{\sqrt{1-4x^2}} $$
Problem 2
Differentiate $y = x \arctan x$ with respect to $x$.
  1. Use the product rule:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \arctan x + x \cdot \frac{1}{1+x^2} $$
  2. So,
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \arctan x + \frac{x}{1+x^2} $$
Problem 3
Find $\displaystyle \frac{d}{dx} \arccos(\sqrt{x})$ for $0 < x < 1$.
  1. Let $y = \arccos(\sqrt{x})$. By the chain rule:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(\sqrt{x})^2}} \cdot \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} = -\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}\sqrt{1-x}} $$
Problem 4
Differentiate $y = \arctan\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$ for $x > 0$.
  1. Let $y = \arctan\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$. By the chain rule:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{1+\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^2} \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{x^2}\right) = \frac{-1}{x^2 + 1} $$
Problem 5
Find $\displaystyle \frac{d}{dx} \left[ x^2 \arcsin x \right]$.
  1. Use the product rule:
    $$ \frac{d}{dx} \left[ x^2 \arcsin x \right] = 2x \arcsin x + x^2 \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} $$
Problem 6
Differentiate $y = \arccos(3x^2)$ for $|x| < \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.
  1. Let $y = \arccos(3x^2)$. By the chain rule:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(3x^2)^2}} \cdot 6x = -\frac{6x}{\sqrt{1-9x^4}} $$
Problem 7
Find $\displaystyle \frac{d}{dx} \arctan(e^x)$.
  1. Let $y = \arctan(e^x)$. By the chain rule:
    $$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{1+(e^x)^2} \cdot e^x = \frac{e^x}{1+e^{2x}} $$

Integrals Involving Inverse Trigonometric Functions

The integrals involving inverse trigonometric functions arise from a wide range of complicated rational functions that are difficult to integrate using other elementary methods. The following problems and solutions are designed to help you practice these integrals.

Problem 1
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \, dx$.
  1. This is a standard integral involving the inverse sine function:
    $$ \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \, dx = \arcsin x + C $$
Problem 2
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{1+x^2} \, dx$.
  1. This is a standard integral involving the inverse tangent function:
    $$ \int \frac{1}{1+x^2} \, dx = \arctan x + C $$
Problem 3
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{|x|\sqrt{x^2-1}} \, dx$ for $|x|>1$.
  1. This is a standard integral involving the inverse secant function:
    $$ \int \frac{1}{|x|\sqrt{x^2-1}} \, dx = \arcsec |x| + C $$
Problem 4
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{2}{4 + x^2} \, dx$.
  1. Rewrite $4 + x^2 = 2^2 + x^2$ and use the formula $\int \frac{1}{a^2 + x^2} dx = \frac{1}{a} \arctan\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) + C$:
    $$ \int \frac{2}{4 + x^2} dx = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \arctan\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) + C = \arctan\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) + C $$
Problem 5
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int_0^{1/2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \, dx$.
  1. The antiderivative is $\arcsin x$. Thus,
    $$ \int_0^{1/2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} dx = \arcsin\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) - \arcsin(0) = \frac{\pi}{6} - 0 = \frac{\pi}{6} $$
Problem 6
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{9 - x^2}} \, dx$.
  1. Let $a = 3$, so use $\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}} dx = \arcsin\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) + C$:
    $$ \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{9 - x^2}} dx = \arcsin\left(\frac{x}{3}\right) + C $$
Problem 7
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{x^2 + 6x + 10} \, dx$.
  1. Complete the square: $x^2 + 6x + 10 = (x+3)^2 + 1$.
    $$ \int \frac{1}{(x+3)^2 + 1} dx = \arctan(x+3) + C $$
Problem 8
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{4x - x^2}} \, dx$ for $0 < x < 4$.
  1. Complete the square: $4x - x^2 = -(x^2 - 4x) = -(x^2 - 4x + 4 - 4) = -[(x-2)^2 - 4] = 4 - (x-2)^2$.
    $$ \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{4x - x^2}} dx = \arcsin\left(\frac{x-2}{2}\right) + C $$
Problem 9
Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{x^2 - 4x + 5} \, dx$.
  1. Complete the square: $x^2 - 4x + 5 = (x-2)^2 + 1$.
    $$ \int \frac{1}{(x-2)^2 + 1} dx = \arctan(x-2) + C $$
Problem 10
Evaluate the definite integral $\displaystyle \int_0^1 \frac{1}{1 + x^2} \, dx$.
  1. The antiderivative is $\arctan x$:
    $$ \int_0^1 \frac{1}{1 + x^2} dx = \arctan(1) - \arctan(0) = \frac{\pi}{4} - 0 = \frac{\pi}{4} $$
Problem 11
Evaluate the definite integral $\displaystyle \int_1^2 \frac{1}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}} \, dx$.
  1. The antiderivative is $\arcsin\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)$:
    $$ \int_1^2 \frac{1}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}} dx = \arcsin(1) - \arcsin\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{3} $$
